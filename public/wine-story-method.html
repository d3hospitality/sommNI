Know what belongs to the bottle.

Our growing collective catalog offers a reading path through its place, label and tasting. Chapters distinguish their evidence so a regional story does not become an invented producer biography.

Sourced context

Producer, appellation and regional-body references support the specific facts in their chapter. The link and review date appear beside the text. Shared regional chapters provide context; they do not establish the vineyard or production method of every wine from that region.

Catalogue reference

Tasting notes are inherited reference descriptions, not a claim that wineLENS tasted your bottle or independently verified every blend. Exact vintages are usually unspecified. The old unsourced historical anecdotes have been withdrawn. Confirmed identity mistakes were corrected; affected sensory descriptions were withdrawn where the old wine identity was wrong.

Editorial guidance

Label-reading questions, comparison exercises and service practice are written by wineLENS. They help you use the sourced context and form your own observations. They are not independent evidence for an estate history, blend, production duration, award or drinking window.

Keep the complete identity

Record producer, cuvée, origin, harvest year and, where relevant, edition, format or disgorgement. Bottle artwork can show a different vintage. A generic entry should not inherit the technical sheet of a more specific release just because its producer and grape match.
